V1 Map Gameplay Review

This page reviews gameplay intent for the two novel V1 map concepts. It is about decisions, risk, and readability, not final art.

Ring Basin Gameplay Review

A bowl-control map where high balconies shoot down into circular recovery tiers and low seats can lob back upward.

Movement

  • red high balcony to basin lip: High red can move toward cluster angles without crossing a gap.
  • blue high balcony to basin lip: High blue mirrors the route so the map does not favor one side.

Danger

  • center low basin: Low ground should feel recoverable, not like an instant-loss pit.
  • outer bowl lips: Crater damage here can turn a safe bowl into a bunge threat.

Destructible Focus

  • left ring lip: Breaking this lip changes rolling and cluster behavior into the basin.
  • right ring lip: The mirrored lip creates a readable target without deleting a spawn.
  • center basin floor: Repeated hits deepen the arena center but should not one-shot a player.

Opening Reads

  • low red shoots up: Shows whether a lower player can threaten a high balcony.
  • high red shoots down: Tests whether high ground feels powerful but not automatic.

Why It Could Be Fun

  • Circular bowls make terrain damage easy to understand because craters visually compound the basin shape.
  • Kaelii-style rolling shots and Perlah-style cluster shots have natural targets without needing extra weapon rules.
  • High and low seats create a real turn-one aiming decision rather than only left-to-right firing.

Risks To Watch

  • Closed rings would be confusing for movement and collision, so v1 should use broken arcs and bowls instead.
  • The low basin must remain playable; if it becomes a trap with no useful shots, the map will feel punitive.
  • Outer balcony spawns need enough width that early terrain damage does not erase a player before they act.

Bridgeworks Gameplay Review

A broken-span map where players fight over bridge lanes while spawns remain on wider, safer ledges.

Movement

  • red low seat to low bridge: A player can contest the bridge, but does not spawn on it.
  • blue low seat to low bridge: Mirrored low bridge access supports 2v2 pressure from both sides.

Danger

  • low broken span: The lower bridge should invite bunge plays without forcing everyone onto narrow land.
  • center air gap: The central gap makes wind and vertical aiming matter.

Destructible Focus

  • high center bridge: Damage here can open a new vertical lane without instantly ending the match.
  • left low bridge: Breaking the bridge lip creates a bunge setup against low-seat movement.
  • right low bridge: The mirrored low bridge keeps the tactical puzzle fair.

Opening Reads

  • low red shoots up: Tests a steep lob through the bridge lattice.
  • high red shoots down: Tests whether high-side ledges can threaten bridge contesters.

Why It Could Be Fun

  • Many spans create more target choices than a single central bridge.
  • Destroying bridge lips changes movement and bunge pressure in a way playtesters can understand immediately.
  • The map has strong identity while still using normal v1 terrain rules.

Risks To Watch

  • If bridge spans are too thin, normal splash damage will make the map feel random and brittle.
  • Players should not spawn on the narrowest bridges; the bridges are objectives, not starting traps.
  • Too many overlapping spans can make projectile paths hard to read, so the visual style must preserve clear air lanes.
